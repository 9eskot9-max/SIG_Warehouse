## System principles

- ERPNext owns approved accounting, stock, Employee and business transaction records. Source systems retain their originating evidence; an import or draft is not proof of posting.
- Use native ERP documents and existing workflows. An integration can stage, link and report evidence without becoming another ledger. Stable source identity, idempotency, conflict handling and read-back are required for writers.
- Drafting, approval, submission and external delivery are separate actions. Preserve role and record permissions. Follow [deployment permission guide](../docs/deployment_permission_guide.md), the [permission incident register](../docs/permission_incident_register.md), and [learning loop](../docs/erpnext_learning_loop.md) for any change.
- **Deployed** means supported by a dated release record; **owner-reported** means not independently verified; **local/staged** means not established as live; **not tested** means the actual workflow or persona remains unaccepted. Do not infer message delivery or user acceptance from a successful deployment.

## How the business system fits together

SIG carries out field work under client agreements and purchase orders, moves materials to sites, records field evidence, completes handover and invoices, then reconciles client receipts and company payments. ERPNext holds the business records and ledgers; RV80, other Excel workbooks, Am and external services capture or prepare particular facts and documents. The same Site, Project/PO, WO, Employee, Item and Supplier identities connect these parts. Those connections are sometimes incomplete or many-to-many: a developer must examine the actual links and exceptions before calculating balances or automating decisions.

```text
Client agreement / PO → Project + site + WO → Site Cycle → field evidence → handover → Sales Invoice
                                              ↑                ↑                         ↓
                                    TCN / visits / Tags     MIR / stock issue     TAWAL / SNB receipt
                                                                  ↑
Supplier → quotation / purchasing → PO → Purchase Receipt → ERP stock → issue / return
Employee → attendance + custody + petty cash → review → Finance accounting → bank evidence
```

This is a conceptual map, not a promise that every arrow is automatically linked or that every historical record has a complete identity. Project supplies the business scope; a physical Site is not a Cost Center. A Site Cycle represents a work episode for a site and its WO where known, with an agreed compatible no-WO path. Multiple scopes, visits, issues, invoices or payments can relate to the same site. The proper identity and child-row references matter more than row positions, similar names or a shared site code.

## Plans page: weekly PM check — deployed 4 November 2026, mode Off

Status: deployed to `neosys.frappe.cloud` on 4 November 2026 (SIG Warehouse `76927313`) in **Off** mode. No PM has been contacted and no reviews exist. Before switching to Test or Live, Operations must fill in `SIG PM Weekly Scope` and confirm each PM's role can read cycles and set decisions. Saved PM plans remain deferred in the implementation plan; this stage needs only the weekly planner snapshot.

**Concept and where it is saved.**
- **Scope** — `SIG PM Weekly Scope`: which Projects each PM covers (one row per PM and project, maintained by Operations). The PM-to-project link did not exist in the data before this.
- **Plan snapshot** — `SIG Weekly Plan Snapshot` (one per week, keyed by the Sunday date): the planner's trip stops, saved by the Sunday job so next week can compare against them.
- **Review** — `SIG PM Weekly Review` (one per PM per week): Box 1 (last week's planned sites, visited flag, reason), Box 2 (this week's sites with Must/Hold/Remove), additions (visits outside the plan), the system comment, the trust score and the Mapping line. Box 2 decisions are written to the existing `SIG Plan Decision` through the planner's own endpoint when the PM saves.
- **Sends** — the existing `Maytapi Message Log`. Mode and send hour are on `SIG Field Ops Settings` ("Weekly PM check"), default Off.

**Purpose.** Give each PM one weekly check that replaces status calls and meeting preparation, and compare the plan with what was visited.

**Rhythm.** Once a week, on Sunday (job `sig_warehouse.sig_operations.pm_weekly.run_scheduled`, hourly, acts once per week from the send hour), which starts the working week (Sunday–Thursday). The three messages below go to each PM together. The send time is not critical; morning is fine. Answers are read from ERP, not from WhatsApp replies (inbound automation remains deferred). No reminder is sent. The answers feed the Sunday operating review (pitch A1).

**The three messages, refreshed each Sunday.**

1. **Last week — focus.** Last week's planned sites, each marked visited or not visited from Site Visits and Site Cycles. Only the misses need an answer:
   - reason (select): access, material, team, TCN, scope or WO, other;
   - optional short note.
   Sites visited that were not on the plan are listed too, so the PM can confirm them as additions.

2. **This week — target.** The planner's proposal for the PM's area this week. For each row the PM chooses Must, Hold or Remove, which writes to the existing SIG Plan Decision record. The PM can add a site the planner does not know, with a reason. Optional line: "WOs received this week updated in Mapping? Y/N".

3. **System comment on the plan.** The system's own short comment on last week's plan: how many planned sites were visited, the misses by reason, and the additions. The PM replies with a trust score from 1 to 5 and one sentence. Tracked week by week to show whether the plan is trusted.

**Messages (staged, Arabic and English).**
- Message 1: "مرحباً {name}، مراجعة الأسبوع الماضي: {link1}" / "Hello {name}, last week's review: {link1}"
- Message 2: "هدف هذا الأسبوع: {link2}" / "This week's target: {link2}"
- Message 3: "تعليق النظام على الخطة: {link3}، وأرسل تقييمك من 1 إلى 5 مع جملة واحدة." / "System comment on the plan: {link3}. Send your score from 1 to 5 with one sentence."

## Operations: from scope to handover and invoice

Operations works across Projects, Sites, Site Cycles, work orders, visits and evidence. The Workbench and Site Cycles are views of the same operational facts. A cycle stage summarizes progress; it does not replace the underlying evidence, warehouse quantities, Sales Invoice or Finance records. Shared PM/coordinator work belongs to the eligible group; an individually assigned native ToDo is a separate follow-up.

| Stage | Business meaning and normal evidence |
|---|---|
| Initial | Establish scope and readiness. Mapping or BOQ can open successor planning work while Initial remains the last attained checkpoint; both are not universally mandatory. |
| Plan | Prepare field work: TCN, MIR and visit as applicable. Tags can also be Plan work when needed for handover. Delay is a review signal on existing work, not a reason to create duplicate tasks. |
| Handover | Recorded preparation reaches the applicable evidence threshold: TCN + MIR + visit + Tags, or TCN + MIR + two accepted visits on the same cycle. Tags and As-Built Operational can proceed in parallel. Photos are evidence/history, not automatically a task. |
| Invoicing | Handover-document work has begun and invoice close-out follows. As-Built BOQ file work belongs here; the actual Sales Invoice and its submission are native accounting records. |
| Closed | Terminal operational state, with closure governed by the applicable normal workflow and evidence. Historic invoice-intake rules have their own recorded behavior; do not infer a universal age-based closure rule. |

Evidence should identify the actual site, Project/PO, WO or compatible cycle and what occurred. A TCN, a visit, an MIR and an invoice prove different things; a generated document is not proof of a client approval or a completed visit. Preserve separate events and timestamps, handle repeated evidence idempotently, and show unresolved identity rather than linking an event to the wrong cycle. Project/family rosters help route shared work; they are not proof of a permanent per-site team assignment.

The current Field Planner uses ERP cycle candidates, team information, positions and site geography to suggest a schedule. Teams move from their latest usable position and continue from their planned stops; travel and capacity matter. PM decisions and TCN date eligibility influence the suggestion. Uncovered dates are provisional up to the documented cutoff. A planning estimate is neither actual arrival nor authorization to enter a site. RV80 remains the existing TCN document workflow. ## Materials, warehouse and purchasing

A field material requirement is related to the work context, item, quantity and UOM. The MIR/Material Request describes demand; warehouse dispatch and stock transactions describe what was actually issued. An MIR can be fulfilled in parts and by several dispatches, so undelivered quantity must remain distinguishable from issued material. Warehouse stock and stock ledger entries are authoritative for physical ERP movements; a request, planned visit or supplier quotation does not itself move stock. Return and custody records must retain the link to the original issue and line where the current process supports it.

Warehouse codes are the preferred material-code vocabulary. Resolve an existing Item by code or description before creating another; use explicit UOM conversion and flag uncertainty rather than silently changing quantity. The ERP Purchasing & Receiving interface is intended to let users select existing suppliers and items, create missing ones when permitted, and work with native Material Requests, Purchase Orders and Purchase Receipts. A suggested AI item description/code is a proposal against the approved catalog rules, not an automatically trusted item or a new stock authority. A supplier VAT number may be entered when known. The editable prompt and existing OpenAI configuration support tuning; actual user permissions, validation and acceptance remain separate.

A Purchase Order expresses an order/commitment. A Purchase Receipt records goods received and valuation; a Purchase Invoice records the supplier bill and liability. Goods can arrive before price is known: use the existing receipt-pricing completion workflow and Finance's valuation rules. Supplier payment and invoice allocation follow the Finance process. Do not treat an SNB transfer as proof of the item, rate, quantity or VAT on a receipt. The existing MIR dispatch and Warehouse Workbench are the operating surfaces to reuse when improving these flows.

For returns, the recorded policy gives Warehouse ten calendar days from a DN's ERP posting date to declare a return against each DN and line. Starting day 11, eligible undeclared balances are closed operationally as no return/consumed, without a stock movement; this does not prove physical consumption. Preserve recorded returns and custody allocations. The policy has an effective marker and a backdated-DN review path; late physical returns need a reasoned correction. A canary closure was observed, but that alone does not prove universal scheduler or workbook acceptance. Owner direction treats import-period quantities as settled, so focus on specific troubled post-import lines. ABDraft is a working draft; final As-Built completion remains operator-owned.

## Integration behavior for future developers

Use a recognizable source record and, where needed, source line and operation identity, then store the ERP document and child-row links returned by the write. The same key and same payload should resolve as an existing result; the same key with different facts is a conflict for review. A timeout requires a status/read-back check before retrying. Record meaningful errors and who can resolve them; do not turn normal waiting, a missing match or a partially fulfilled request into a false success.

Separate captured, validated, draft, approved, submitted and reconciled states. Fulfilment states such as partial and complete describe quantities, not accounting approval. External provider acceptance is not final delivery. Failed or ambiguous identity, UOM, quantity, access or matching must remain visible for an authorized person to correct. Preserve the origin and timing of evidence and avoid writing a parallel balance that can drift from ERP.

For each change, define the real operator, input, authoritative records, decisions, output and exception path in this document's terms. Inspect deployed source and current data, preserve existing role baselines, and record deployment and actual-user acceptance separately. Keep technical migration detail and full historical logs in linked documents and the legacy register; update this white paper's operating logic and status when a change becomes real.
